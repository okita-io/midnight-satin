Chapter IV The Gilded Cage

The Gilded Cage

Midnight Satin • Vol. 1

The ballroom was a sea of velvet and diamonds, a suffocating display of wealth that made Eleanor’s corset feel tighter than the laces allowed. She stood near the periphery, a shadow in emerald silk, watching the duke from across the room. He was laughing at something the French ambassador said, his head thrown back, exposing the sharp line of his throat.

"He looks dangerous tonight," a voice murmured beside her. Eleanor didn't need to turn to know it was Julian. He smelled of cloves and old books, a comforting scent in this shark tank.

"He is always dangerous, Julian. That is the point of him," she replied, her gaze never wavering. The duke turned then, as if feeling the weight of her stare. His eyes, dark as the onyx ring on his finger, locked onto hers. The laughter died on his lips, replaced by a slow, knowing smirk that sent a shiver down her spine that had nothing to do with the drafty hall.

He began to move through the crowd, parting the sea of aristocrats like a ship cutting through dark waters. Eleanor’s breath hitched. Every step he took was a promise, or perhaps a threat. Julian stiffened beside her. "Eleanor, don't."

"I have to," she whispered, her heart hammering a frantic rhythm against her ribs. She took a step forward, meeting the duke in the center of the room, under the grand chandelier. The crystals caught the light, scattering rainbows over his dark coat.

"My Lady," he said, his voice a low rumble that vibrated through the floorboards. He extended a gloved hand. "I believe this dance is mine."

She hesitated, the weight of a hundred staring eyes pressing down on her. To take his hand was to accept the scandal. To refuse him was to declare war.

His fingers brushed hers, electric even through the kidskin. "Are you afraid?" he murmured, leaning close enough that she could feel the heat radiating from him. "You should be." The music swelled, a haunting waltz that seemed to originate from the shadows themselves.

Eleanor lifted her chin. "I fear nothing in this room, Your Grace. Especially not you." It was a lie, a beautiful, fragile lie, and they both knew it. He smiled, and for a moment, the predator softened.

They began to turn, the world dissolving into a blur of color and light. With every rotation, he pulled her closer, until there was no air left between them.
